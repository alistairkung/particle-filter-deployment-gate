## Bootstrap update and numerical checks

Use the approved transition as the bootstrap proposal, initially equal log weights. For a delivered reading add the joint log likelihood conditional on the previous and proposed states. With input-independent delivery a missing interval adds zero log likelihood. Normalise by log-sum-exp. Compute ESS as one divided by the sum of squared normalised weights. If ESS falls below half the particle count, use systematic resampling with one fresh uniform offset and reset to equal weights. Preserve the weighted posterior before optional resampling for cut forecasts. Record ESS before resampling, maximum weight, unique ancestors and counts per regime after resampling. Recurring regime extinction, unstable estimates across seeds or concentration on a single ancestor triggers development diagnosis. All-zero mass, a nonfinite normaliser or an impossible delivered observation is an explicit failure. Never silently substitute equal weights.

Use deterministic fixtures for an enumerable two-update posterior, an uninformative likelihood, a known likelihood ratio, invariance to a common log-weight offset, missing evidence, fixed-offset resampling and a 0/1 breach endpoint. Compare with exact enumeration on a tiny finite-state model and verify identical states and weights through a common prefix under incompatible evaluator futures. At equal route work the two actions must have identical physical outcomes for each matched forecast future. Verify a signed difference using all four paired cells, including a hold-only breach.

## Numerical budget and reproducibility

First benchmark one prefix and one matched action rollout on the intended machine. Use a predetermined development panel of 12 held-out roots per regime with four independent seeds. Try particle counts 128, 256 and 512, then 1024 only if feasible. Compare adjacent doublings using coupled draws where possible. Select the smaller setting only when the root-mean-square change in cut regime probabilities is at most 0.03 and in cut mean work at most 0.01 seconds, reporting per-regime values and seed dispersion. On the same panel with the chosen K and coupled rollout draws, report the change in mean incremental-risk loss on doubling the particle count alongside its repeat-seed variation. Confirm that no regime exceeds either state tolerance. Freeze the smaller passing particle count. Otherwise advance one doubling or report the resource constraint. If bias or repeated collapse remains material, diagnose before locking.

For fixed posteriors compare independent batches at K = 64, 128, 256, 512 and successive doublings only while feasible. For each output, estimate conditional variance of its forecast mean across seeds, averaged over roots with equal regime weight. Independent unbiased rollout noise adds its variance to expected squared loss. Report that contribution for the signed increment for both full and ablated forecasts. Use the existing 0.03 RMS seed-error tolerance for the two action probabilities and the signed increment on the development panel. Choose the smallest K meeting this numerical stability check at both K and 2K. Report per-regime stability and runtime. Repeat-seed checks target Monte Carlo error conditional on the model. They cannot establish physical calibration. If the numerical thresholds or wall-time target cannot be met, bring the measured trade-off to the owner before any protected access. Both variants use the same locked K. No extra protected roots are earned by drawing further rollouts.
