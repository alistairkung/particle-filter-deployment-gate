# Condition primary action forecasts on regime persistence

The ordinary synthetic evaluation keeps the background intensity and publisher condition at the decision cut in force for the next 120 seconds. This defines the primary forecasting question for the particle-filter study. How long a production workload persists is outside the scope of this assumption.

Before the cut, the filter permits probabilistic regime transitions at every five-second update under a time-homogeneous transition law. It infers the joint posterior over background intensity, unfinished DB work and publisher condition from the available outbox history and permitted controls. The experimental intervention time and condition labels stay private to the evaluator.

At the cut, average over that joint posterior to forecast a write-SLO breach separately under 25% hold and 60% promote. In a sampled forecast, start both actions from the same posterior draw and keep its background intensity and publisher condition fixed in both continuations. Query and write arrivals, service demands, unfinished DB work and publisher processing continue to evolve. Every baseline receives the same permitted information and the same future-persistence assumption.

Post-decision regime switching belongs to a separate model-mismatch analysis. Specify its transition probabilities or dwell-time assumptions, justify them independently of protected outcomes, and freeze them before evaluation.
