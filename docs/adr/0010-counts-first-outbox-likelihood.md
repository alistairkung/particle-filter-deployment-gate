# Score each delivered outbox reading with one counts-first joint likelihood

For a five-second interval, let $M$ be completed marks, $N$ ending pending count, $A$ oldest pending age and $D$ median completed commit-to-mark delay. Given the particle's proposed state transition and permitted prior history $C$, use the chain-rule factorisation $p(M\mid C)\,p(N\mid M,C)\,p(A,D\mid M,N,C)$. The identity allows dependence among all four measurements. Each fitted conditional law is an empirical approximation.

Apply this likelihood once to the delivered reading. Throughput is $M/5$. When $N=0$, age is zero with certainty. When $M=0$, delay is undefined and contributes no timing factor. The count law must allow zero marks, with its tail assumption and uncertainty documented. A missing complete reading contributes unit likelihood. Retain measurement and availability times. The starting count available from prior history can condition the transition. The ending count first enters as evidence in its own interval. [Sequential factorisation](0004-particle-workload-and-publisher-memory.md)

Hypothetical claim and mark visits in a particle's trajectory contribute to its FIFO work. The fitted observation law averages over omitted publisher paths given the transition endpoints and permitted context. Its likelihood is the sole score for the observed counts and times. Applying a further match score against the particle's hypothetical marks or pending count would count evidence twice.

Fit and select families using development roots only. Align evaluator regime and unfinished work with each interval's boundaries by replay. Split whole root families for validation. At inference each particle supplies its hypothesised state and receives only delivered outbox history and permitted controls. Keep protected physical state on the evaluator side for scoring. Check empty-outbox frequency, joint count distributions and the conditional age–delay relationship within each regime.
